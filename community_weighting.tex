	\section*{Plots, coverages, and data frames}
	We will demonstrate one-level and two-level community weighting with a single plot. Table~\ref{tab:l1_plot} shows, a plot for the one-level example - there is a single subplot for which plot composition is reported. For the two-level example, Table~\ref{tab:l2_plot} shows a plot with \textit{two} subplots - in general there can be \textit{two or more} subplots per plot. The two-level refers to the fact that weighted traits are first computed at the subplot level and these subplot traits are weighted using subplot weights to produce a plot level trait value. One-level weighting is a special case of two-level weighting, where the there is one subplot. Table~\ref{tab:plots_coverages}, provides the employed notation. $A, B, C, D$ represent the cover classes (e.g. species, plant functional types). In Tables~\ref{tab:l1_plot},~\ref{tab:l2_plot}, $A_{r}^{s}$ represents the $r$-th replicate for cover class $A$ in the $s$-th subplot. In Table~\ref{tab:l2_plot}, there are $2$ replicates for cover class $A$ in subplot $1$ ($A_{1}^1, A_{2}^1$) and $2$ replicates for $D$ in subplot $2$ ($D_{1}^1, D_{2}^1$). The cover weight (\texttt{CoverWeight} column) for the various cover classes for the subplots are reported in Tables~\ref{tab:l1_cover},~\ref{tab:l2_cover}. $c_{k}^{s}$, represents the coverage weight for cover class $k$ in subplot $s$. Note that we include subplot identifier in the superscript!

	\section*{Community weighting and sampling}
	In what follows we will use the following notation for sampled values:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item 
		${[\widetilde{c_k^s}]}_i$: $i$-th sample from a specified distribution $D_c(c_k^s)$. 
		\item 
		${[\widetilde{t_{k_r}^s}]}_i$: $i$-th sample from a specified distribution $D_t(\mu_{k_r}^s, \sigma_{k_r}^s)$.
	\end{itemize} 
	
	The community weighted trait for a plot can be obtained as follows:
	\begin{itemize} 
		\item For $i \in {1, \ldots, M}$:
		\begin{itemize} 
			\item For each subplot $s$:
			\begin{itemize}
				\item For every \texttt{CoverID}~$=k$:
				\begin{itemize} 
					\item Choose: $r$ (if replicates exist for \texttt{CoverID}).
					\item Generate: $({[\widetilde{c_k^s}]}_i, {[\widetilde{t_{k_r}^s}]}_i)$.
				\end{itemize}
				\item Compute: ${[t^s]}_i = \frac{\sum_k{{[\widetilde{c_k^s}]}_i} {[\widetilde{t_{k_r}^s}]}_i}{\sum_k{{[\widetilde{c_k^s}]}_i}}$ (subplot level weighting). 
			\end{itemize} 
		\item Compute $\widetilde{y_i} = \frac{\sum_s{w^s}{{[t^s]}_i}}{\sum_s{w^s}}$ (plot level weighting).
		\end{itemize}
		\item Compute:
		\begin{itemize} 
			\item $\widehat{\mu} = \mbox{\texttt{mean}}(\widetilde{y_1}, \ldots, \widetilde{y_M})$ (community weighted mean), 
			\item $\widehat{\sigma} = \mbox{\texttt{sdev}}(\widetilde{y_1}, \ldots, \widetilde{y_M})$ (community weighted standard deviation).
		\end{itemize}
	\end{itemize}
	\textbf{Example (one-level case):} For a particular $i$, let $r=2$ for $A$ was chosen. 
	${[y^{1}]}_i = {[\widetilde{c_A^{1}}]}_i {[\widetilde{t_{A_2}^{1}}]}_i $
	
	
	Two specifications, \textit{percent cover} and \textit{ordinal class}, can be employed for \texttt{CoverWeight} in tables~\ref{tab:l1_cover},~\ref{tab:l2_cover}. For percent cover, the value for \texttt{CoverWeight} is the percentage of subplot covered by the \texttt{CoverID}. In the case of ordinal class specification, the \texttt{CoverWeight} value represents the bin index for binned coverage ranges. For ordinal class specification, the bin index is mapped to a specified mode value in the specified bin range.

	\section*{Notes} 
	\begin{itemize}
		\item
		\textbf{Trait uncertainty}: The value used for (say) $t_{A_1}^{\one}$ can be sampled from a distribution specified with columns for mean trait value and the associated standard deviation. In our context, the mean and deviations are typically produced from leaf-level model predictions. The trait standard deviation column is omitted in the tables for clarity. 
		
		\item 
		\textbf{Cover replicate uncertainty}: The two trait values for \texttt{CWTrait} in table~\ref{tab:l1_sample_trait} (and column \texttt{SubsampleTrait} in table~\ref{tab:l1_subsample_trait}) are due to choice of the replicate for \texttt{CoverID} $A$. This is ``specified" by the number of replicates for each \texttt{CoverID}. 
		
		\item
		\textbf{Cover weight uncertainty}: Ordinal cover classes and percent cover are two approaches for specifying \texttt{Coverweight} (e.g. $c_{A}^{\one}$). Ordinal cover class specification requires binning the range $[0, 100]$ and the bin membership for the considered \texttt{CoverID}. For percent cover, \texttt{CoverID} is specified as a percentage (range: $[0, 100]$) or a proportion (range: $[0, 1]$). Typically, human visual estimates are used for the \texttt{CoverWeight}, and the imprecision of human observation leads to uncertainties in \texttt{CoverWeight}.   
		
%		\item 
%		\textbf{Sampling \texttt{CWTrait}}: Propagating the \texttt{CWTrait} uncertainty is critical for robust model training and evaluation. Specifically, \texttt{CWTrait} ($\widetilde{y}$) can be computed using sampled values for $t_{E}^{s}$ and $c_{E}^{s}$, where $s$ is \texttt{SubplotID} (say $\one$) and $c$ is the \texttt{CoverID} (e.g., $A$ for $w$ and $A_1$ for $t$). The spectra-trait pair $(X, \widetilde{y})$ is used for model training, with different sampled values of $\widetilde{y}$ for a \texttt{SubplotID} during various folds of cross-validation.      
		
		\item 
		\textbf{Montecarlo \texttt{CWTrait} estimation}:
		Monte-Carlo estimation provides a general, tractable estimate of \texttt{CWTrait} (${y}_{\mu}^{cw}$) (and its standard deviation, $y_{\sigma}^{cw}$). Given a dataset and distributions for $t_c^{s}$ and $c_c^{s}$ (see below), we can compute $\{\widetilde{y_1}, \ldots \widetilde{y_M}\}$ ($\widetilde{y_i}$ uses samples  $(\widetilde{(t_c^{s})_i}$ $\widetilde{(c_c^{s})_i})$ in the formulas in the tables). The $\{\widetilde{y_i}\}$ are reduced to yield $y_{\mu}^{cw}$ and $y_{\sigma}^{cw}$. The $y_{\mu}^{cw}$ is used as the ``true" value for model evaluation and in the predicted-vs-true plot; $y_{\sigma}^{cw}$ provides the error bar for the ``true" value in a predicted-vs-true plot; and sample with mean $y_{\mu}^{cw}$ and standard deviation $y_{\sigma}^{cw}$ is used during model training.
		
		\item 
		\textbf{Ordinal \texttt{CoverWeight} specification}: 
		\begin{itemize}
			\item
			Bin index (integer) $i \in \{ 1, \ldots B\}$.
			\item 
			The end points for the $B$ bins $e_0, e_1, \ldots, e_B$, $e_b \in [0, 100]$; bin mode offsets, $o_1, \ldots o_B \in [0, 1]$; and bin concentrations for ``peakiness", $k_1, \ldots, k_B$ (table~\ref{tab:ordinal_cover}). The ``representative" value for bin $b$ is $r_b = e_{b - 1} + o_b(e_b - e_{b - 1})$. 
			\begin{table}[htbp]
				\centering
				\caption{Specification for ordinal cover}
				\label{tab:ordinal_cover}
				\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|Sc|}
					\hline 
					\texttt{BinIndex} & \texttt{LeftEnd} & \texttt{RightEnd} & \texttt{Offset} & \texttt{Concentration}\\ \hline 
					$1$  & $e_0$ & $e_1$ & $o_1$ & $k_1$ \\ \hline
					$\cdots$ & $\cdots$ & $\cdots$ & $\cdots$ & $\cdots$ \\ \hline
					$B$  & $e_{B-1}$ & $e_B$ & $o_B$ & $k_B$ \\ \hline
				\end{tabular}
			\end{table}	
			\item
			A mode $r_b$, concentration $k_b$, support $[\texttt{max}(0, r_{b - 1}), \texttt{min}(r_{b + 1}, 100)]$, \texttt{Beta} distribution is used for sampling $\widetilde{(c_c^{s})_i}$, if \texttt{CoverWeight} = $b$. 
		\end{itemize} 
					
				
					
		\item
		\textbf{Percent \texttt{CoverWeight} specification}:  
		\begin{itemize}
			\item
			Percentage (integer) $p \in [0, 100]$. 
			\item 
			The end points for $B$ bins: $e_0, e_1, \ldots, e_B \in [0, 100]$; and uncertainties $u_1, \ldots u_B \in [0, 100]$. $u_b$ is the uncertainty, if $p \in [e_{b - 1}, e_b)$ and corresponds to one standard deviation. 
			\begin{table}[htbp]
				\centering
				\caption{Specification for percent cover}
				\label{tab:percent_cover}
				\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|Sc|}
					\hline 
					\texttt{BinIndex} & \texttt{LeftEnd} & \texttt{RightEnd} & \texttt{Uncertainty} \\ \hline 
					$1$  & $e_0$ & $e_1$ & $u_1$ \\ \hline
					$\cdots$ & $\cdots$ & $\cdots$ & $\cdots$ \\ \hline
					$B$  & $e_{B-1}$ & $e_B$ & $u_B$ \\ \hline
				\end{tabular}
			\end{table}
			\item 
			A \texttt{TruncNormal} with mode $p$ and range $[\texttt{max}(0, p - u_b), \texttt{min}(p + u_b, 100)]$ is used for sampling $\widetilde{(c_c^{s})_i}$, if $p \in [e_{b - 1}, e_{b})$.
			
		\end{itemize} 
			
	\end{itemize}
